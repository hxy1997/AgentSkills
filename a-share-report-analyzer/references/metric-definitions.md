# Metric Definitions

Use these reminders to keep language and calculations consistent.

## Common metrics

- `PE`: market cap divided by attributable net profit, or share price divided by EPS
- `PB`: market cap divided by book value, or share price divided by book value per share
- `ROE`: attributable net profit divided by average equity
- `ROA`: net profit divided by average assets
- `DCF`: discounted cash flow valuation
- `FCF`: free cash flow
- `real yield`: nominal government-bond yield minus expected inflation; use market-implied real yields when available
- `DXY`: trade-weighted US dollar index; a broad FX driver, not a substitute for the company's actual currency exposure
- `price deck`: explicit bear/base/bull commodity or product-price assumptions used in earnings, NAV, or DCF
- `realized price`: company selling price after timing, mix, treatment charges, hedging, and FX
- `MA`: moving average, usually MA5, MA10, MA20, MA60, MA120, or MA250
- `turnover rate`: traded shares divided by free-float shares for the period
- `margin balance`: financing plus securities-lending balance, used as leverage and crowding evidence
- `dragon-tiger list`: exchange-disclosed abnormal trading list; use it as event and participant evidence
- `block trade premium/discount`: block-trade price divided by reference market price minus 1
- `lockup expiry`: restricted shares becoming tradable; use market-cap ratio and date to judge supply pressure
- `shareholder count`: number of shareholder accounts; falling count may imply concentration, rising count may imply dispersion

## Interpretation reminders

- A metric is only useful if it matches the sector economics.
- `PE` can be misleading when earnings are cyclical, distorted, or near an inflection.
- `PB` is often more useful for financials and some asset-heavy sectors.
- `PS` can be useful when margins are immature but revenue quality matters.
- `DCF` needs credible cash-flow visibility. Do not use it mechanically.
- K-line and capital-flow metrics are timing, sentiment, liquidity, and risk indicators. They are not intrinsic-value anchors by themselves.
- Macro-news and policy evidence must be translated into explicit variables before use: demand, price, cost, margin, capex, financing cost, discount rate, regulation, supply chain, or risk appetite.
- Separate nominal rates from real rates and expected policy from surprise policy. Do not use “rate hike bearish / rate cut bullish” as the final conclusion.
- For commodity businesses, separate spot, realized, and cycle-normalized prices; use a price deck rather than a generic earnings haircut.
- Prevent double counting: if a policy shock already changes the commodity price deck, do not apply a second unexplained policy haircut to earnings. A separately disclosed discount-rate change is allowed.
- Broker-report ratings and target prices are sourced opinions. Use their assumptions and revisions, not the rating label alone.
- Official announcements are primary-source facts, but still need execution and financial-statement reconciliation.

## Financial-sector reminders

- `银行`: emphasize `PB`, `ROE`, capital adequacy, provisioning, and margin quality.
- `券商`: emphasize `PB`, cycle-aware `PE`, risk-control indicators, and revenue structure.
- `保险`: emphasize solvency, reserve adequacy, underwriting quality, investment contribution, and sector-appropriate valuation anchors.

## Industry-overlay reminder

Before choosing metrics, ask:

- Is this metric structurally informative for this sub-industry?
- Is there a better sector-specific metric?
- Does the market usually price this business on earnings, assets, sales, or long-duration value?
