# Sector Overlay: Insurance

Use this reference for insurers. Distinguish life versus property and casualty when relevant.

## Why the generic framework is not enough

Insurers combine an underwriting business with an investment business. Solvency, reserve adequacy, and investment-return assumptions matter as much as reported earnings.

## Financial-analysis focus

Prioritize:

- business mix: life, health, property and casualty
- premium growth quality
- underwriting profitability
- investment-return contribution
- reserve adequacy
- solvency adequacy
- embedded value or similar long-duration value logic when relevant

## Property and casualty lens

Pay attention to:

- combined ratio
- loss ratio
- expense ratio
- catastrophe exposure

## Life-insurance lens

Pay attention to:

- new business value or similar value-of-new-business indicators
- product mix
- interest-rate sensitivity
- liability duration and investment matching

## Cash-flow and earnings caution

Reported earnings alone are not enough. Reserve movement, investment assumptions, and liability valuation can distort simple year-on-year comparisons.

## Valuation reminders

Common anchors:

- `PB`
- embedded-value-style discussion when applicable
- dividend capacity
- adjusted return-on-equity logic

## Current-regulation reminder

Solvency rules are regulatory and can change. Verify current solvency definitions and thresholds when they are used in the conclusion.
